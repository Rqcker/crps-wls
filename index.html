Paper

Abstract

Blockchain oracle networks, including decentralised physical infrastructure networks (DePINs), report physical measurements from a source that can manipulate the underlying process. A carbon project operator, for example, can dry the soil to raise its apparent soil organic carbon (SOC) and earn credit for carbon it never stored. Existing peer prediction mechanisms cover discrete signals under a strategic source, or continuous probabilistic reports under an honest source, but not continuous reports under a strategic source. We close this gap for Gaussian reports with continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) peer scoring that makes truthful reporting a strict equilibrium under a flat prior. Our main result is an if-and-only-if characterisation: report-based rules identify and deter source manipulation precisely when the sensors are diverse (when their types respond differently to the same manipulation). When diversity fails, no report-based mechanism can deter the source, whatever its scoring or penalty. A uniform shift of every report mimics a genuine rise in SOC and, by CRPS translation equivariance, leaves every peer score unchanged. With eight SOC models calibrated on 37,280 real European soil spectra, baseline aggregators over-credit by up to 57% under manipulation, whereas the mechanism stays unbiased and sizes a closed-form on-chain stake. As a deterministic function of the reports, it runs on a DePIN as a smart contract that issues credits and slashes the source stake without a trusted aggregator. The credited value still carries the sensor's calibration error, so the guarantee is manipulation invariance rather than absolute accuracy.

Theorem · Source deterrence if and only if sensor diversity

In the flat-prior Gaussian reporting model, CRPS peer scoring makes truthful reporting a strict best response at the honest-source equilibrium. Fix truthful reports by non-colluding reporters. A penalty P then exists for which s = 0 is a strict source best response if and only if the sensor sensitivities αi are not all equal. When they are all equal, a manipulation at θ0 and an honest source at the higher value θ0 + ᾱs induce exactly the same report law, so no admissible settlement rule can tell them apart, whatever its scoring or penalty.

Mechanism

How it works

  1. Report. Each sensor i submits a Gaussian belief N(mi, vi) about the parcel's SOC. Its mean responds to a manipulation s through a measured sensitivity αi.
  2. Score. Each sensor is paid the negative closed-form Gaussian CRPS of its forecast against its peers' reports. CRPS is strictly proper, so the truthful mean and variance are the unique best response; no ground truth is ever needed.
  3. Read out. Weighted least squares on the design [1 | α] separates the estimate θ̂ from the manipulation ŝ. Credits are issued on θ̂; the source's stake is slashed when ŝ clears a 5% false-alarm threshold. Both are deterministic in the reports, so the whole round runs as a smart contract.

Scope

Beyond soil carbon

The theorem is not about soil. It is about payment settled on a continuous measurement that the paid party can stage before it is taken. Any domain with that shape admits the same test, and the test asks five things of it.

Measured here

Soil carbon MRV

Credits settle on estimated SOC. The paper measures how eight spectral models respond when the site state is staged before reading.

Candidate

Water-quality credits

A continuous concentration or load can be moved by timing, flushing or dilution. The test asks whether independent readers move differently.

Candidate

Methane-intensity penalties

Operations can change around an observation window. The mechanism needs readers with different physical or temporal sensitivities.

Boundary

Identical meters fail

If every reader is the same instrument or model, reports alone cannot separate staging from a real increase. Add a modality or obtain trusted truth.

Continuous settlement
A payment, penalty or contractual trigger settles on a continuously measured quantity, not a yes-or-no attestation.
No cheap truth
There is no affordable trusted ground truth. Where a certified meter settles the question, use that meter.
Independent readers
At least three independently operated estimators read the same physical event. Two is the algebraic minimum; three is the practical floor.
A spread of responses
The readers respond to the staging move by different amounts. Different modalities give this almost for free; a fleet of identical instruments never does.
Staging, not tampering
The manipulation acts on the physical process before measurement. Falsifying a report afterwards is a different, better-studied problem.
